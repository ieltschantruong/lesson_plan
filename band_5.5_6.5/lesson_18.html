Speaking Practice

Topic: Animals

💬 Part 1

  1. Do you like animals?
  2. What's your favorite animal/ Why?
  3. Are people in your country fond of animals?
  4. Do many people in your country keep a pet?
  5. Do children like animals?
  6. Did you have any pets when you were a child?
💡 View P1 Sample Answers ▼

1. Do you like animals?

Yes, absolutely. I'm a huge animal lover. I find them incredibly adorable and fascinating to watch.

2. What's your favorite animal/ Why?

My favorite animal is the dog. They are wonderfully affectionate companion animals that always show loyalty to their owners.

3. Are people in your country fond of animals?

Yes, I believe so. Many people here treat domestic animals like dogs and cats as members of their own families.

4. Do many people in your country keep a pet?

Yes, keeping a pet is very common. Most households have at least one small mammal to keep them company.

5. Do children like animals?

Children generally love animals. They are naturally drawn to pets with soft fur and enjoy visiting zoos to see various reptiles and birds.

6. Did you have any pets when you were a child?

Yes, I had a puppy when I was young. He was so adorable and playful, and taking care of him taught me a lot of responsibility.

🎙️ Part 2 Cue Card

1 Min Prep | 2 Mins Speak

Describe an interesting wild animal.

You should say:

  • What it looks like
  • When you saw/ knew about it for the first time
  • Where it lives
  • And explain why it is interesting
💡 View P2 Sample Answer ▼
"I'd like to talk about the Giant Panda, which is a fascinating wild mammal native to China.

It has a very distinct appearance with its thick black and white fur, round ears, and black patches around its eyes, making it look incredibly adorable. Despite their cuddly look, they have strong claws and paws to help them climb.

I first knew about them from a wildlife documentary when I was a child, and I was instantly captivated.

They primarily live in the cool, wet bamboo forests of central China, high up in the mountains.

What makes them so interesting is their diet. Even though they biologically belong to the carnivore order and have the digestive system for meat, they act almost entirely as herbivores, surviving mostly on bamboo. Their playful, seemingly affectionate nature makes them a globally recognized symbol of wildlife conservation."
