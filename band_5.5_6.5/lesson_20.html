📝 Notes: Turnover vs Revenue vs Sales

Three money words that are easy to confuse in business texts.

turnover/ˈtɜːnəʊvər/(doanh thu (Anh-Anh))

Meaning: the total value of goods/services a company sells in a period (UK usage; very close to "revenue" in the US).

Focus: the money coming in from selling, before costs are taken out. Also: staff turnover = how many employees leave and are replaced.

The company's turnover rose to £2 million last year.

revenue/ˈrevənjuː/(thu nhập / tổng doanh thu)

Meaning: all the income a company receives, usually from its main business but also from other sources (fees, interest, investments).

Focus: the widest "money in" word; used everywhere, especially in US English and in finance. Revenue − costs = profit.

The firm's revenue comes from ticket sales and advertising.

sales/seɪlz/(doanh số / việc bán hàng)

Meaning: the act of selling, or the number/amount of products sold.

Focus: the activity or the quantity sold, not the whole company income. Often plural: sales figures, sales increased, direct sales.

Sales of electric cars doubled this year.

💡 Quick tip: Sales are what you sell → turnover is the total value of those sales in the UK → revenue is all the money that comes in → profit is what is left after costs. Note: the workbook gives simplified definitions for turnover and revenue, so use this note for the finer differences.

Speaking Practice

Topic: Saving Money

💬 Part 1: Save Money

  1. Did you save money when you were a child?
  2. Do your parents give you money for house cleaning?
  3. Have you given money to other children?
  4. Do you think parents should teach children to save money?
  5. What are the ways to improve money management skills?
  6. Is it acceptable to borrow money?
💡 View P1 Sample Answers▼

1. Did you save money when you were a child?

Yes, I did. I kept my coins in a piggy bank, and my parents taught me to save money for a rainy day.

2. Do your parents give you money for house cleaning?

Not really. My parents give me pocket money every week, and helping with housework is simply part of family life.

3. Have you given money to other children?

Yes, occasionally. I gave some to my younger cousin so he could buy snacks at school. I think it taught him to spend money wisely.

4. Do you think parents should teach children to save money?

Absolutely. I believe it's crucial because children who learn early are less likely to be broke or get into debt as adults.

5. What are the ways to improve money management skills?

You could set a monthly budget, track your spending with an app, and put aside a fixed amount each month. Practice builds good money management skills.

6. Is it acceptable to borrow money?

It depends. Borrowing a small amount from friends in an emergency is fine, but borrowing too often can make you get into debt.

🎙️ Part 2 Cue Card

1 Min Prep | 2 Mins Speak

Describe a time when you saved money to buy something.

You should say:

  • What it is
  • When you bought it
  • How you saved money to buy it
  • And say how important it is
🐷💵
💡 View P2 Sample Answer▼
"I'd like to talk about the time I saved money to buy a laptop.

It was a brand new MacBook, and I bought it around two years ago. At that time I was fully occupied with my work when my old laptop suddenly crashed and couldn't be repaired, so I made up my mind to replace it.

When I first laid my eyes on the MacBook, I was struck by its large display and blazing-fast performance. Unfortunately, it cost me a fortune, so I had to save up for quite a long time. Instead of squandering money on exorbitant clothes, I set aside part of my salary every month, and in a little over a year I could finally afford it.

It's important to me because it taught me to spend money wisely and showed that patience pays off. In retrospect, I think it was the best purchase I've ever made."
