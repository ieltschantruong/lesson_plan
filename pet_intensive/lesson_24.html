76 Describe a place with a lot of trees that you would like to visit (e.g. a forest)

Part 2 Cue Card:

You should say: Where it is; How you knew this place; What it is like; And explain why you would like to visit it.

Band 7.5+ Sample Response:

"A place with an abundance of trees that I am dying to visit is the Cuc Phuong National Park in Ninh Binh Province, Vietnam. I first learned about this pristine sanctuary through a nature documentary on national TV. The park is renowned for its dense primeval canopy, ancient thousand-year-old trees, and rich biodiversity. What makes it so appealing is its untouched, serene wilderness where one can immerse oneself in pure nature away from city pollution. I want to visit it because trekking beneath giant ancient banyans offers a deeply rejuvenating, therapeutic escape that restores my mental energy."

Lexical Masterclass:

1. Primeval canopy (noun phrase) /praɪˈmiː.vəl ˈkæn.ə.pi/ - Ancient, untouched forest ceiling created by dense tree tops.

2. Rejuvenating (adjective) /rɪˈdʒuː.vən.eɪ.tɪŋ/ - Making someone feel fresh, energetic, and young again.

3. Sanctuary (noun) /ˈsæŋk.tʃu.ər.i/ - A protected, peaceful place of safety or natural refuge.

Part 3 Extensions (10 Questions):

1. Why do people like visiting places with trees or forests?

Ans: People visit wooded areas to seek psychological respite from urban noise. Forest environments lower cortisol levels and provide fresh oxygen. For instance, weekend hikers often report lower stress after spending hours among pine trees. Therefore, forests serve as natural mental restores.

2. What are the benefits of spending time in nature?

Ans: Spending time outdoors boosts immunity and fosters mindful contemplation. Exposure to natural sunlight enhances vitamin D synthesis and improves mood balance. For example, regular nature walks reduce symptoms of anxiety. Consequently, nature visits improve holistic health.

3. How do forests contribute to environmental protection?

Ans: Forests act as massive carbon sinks and prevent soil erosion during heavy rains. Tree root networks absorb floodwaters, while leaves filter air pollutants. For instance, rainforests regulate global humidity cycles. Thus, forest ecosystems stabilize global climates.

4. Do you think cities should have more green spaces?

Ans: Yes, urban planning must prioritize green corridors to combat the urban heat island effect. Parks provide essential recreational zones for residents. For example, public gardens in Singapore significantly cool local ambient temperatures. Hence, green infrastructure is essential for modern cities.

5. What activities can people do in forests?

Ans: Visitors can engage in eco-tourism activities such as guided bird watching, trail camping, and photography. These activities foster environmental appreciation without harming wildlife. For example, camping under forest canopies teaches survival skills. Therefore, forests offer diverse educational leisure.

6. How has deforestation affected your country?

Ans: Deforestation has heightened risks of flash floods and habitat loss in mountainous regions. Tree loss destabilizes soil structures, triggering severe landslides during monsoon seasons. For instance, hill deforestation led to severe mudslides in central Vietnam. Consequently, clearing forests degrades natural safety buffers.

7. Should governments invest more money in protecting forests?

Ans: Absolutely, state budgets must allocate funding toward forest ranger patrols and re-afforestation projects. Protecting existing reserves prevents costly ecological disasters down the road. For example, anti-poaching units safeguard endangered species. Thus, state investment guarantees ecological survival.

8. What's the difference between visiting a park and visiting a natural forest?

Ans: City parks offer manicured, predictable spaces, whereas natural forests provide untamed biodiversity. Parks feature paved walkways, whereas forests require rugged trekking skills. For instance, walking in a manicured park differs from navigating dense jungle trails. Hence, forests offer deeper wilderness immersion.

9. How can people help protect trees and forests?

Ans: Citizens can reduce consumption of paper goods and support sustainable forestry certifications. Participating in local tree-planting drives directly restores green cover. For example, choosing recycled wood products discourages illegal logging. Therefore, conscious consumer choices protect global forests.

10. Do you think children today spend enough time in nature?

Ans: Unfortunately no, contemporary youth suffer from sedentary indoor lifestyles due to screen addiction. Modern kids spend hours on gaming consoles rather than exploring outdoor parks. For instance, weekend screen time far exceeds outdoor play. Consequently, parents must actively encourage outdoor excursions.

77 Describe an occasion when you lost your way

Part 2 Cue Card:

You should say: Where you were; What happened; How you felt; And explain how you found your way.

Band 7.5+ Sample Response:

"I’d like to recount an occasion when I became completely disoriented in the labyrinthine alleys of Hanoi's Old Quarter during my first solo visit. I was trying to find a famous hidden cafe, but because the streets look nearly identical with dense shopfronts, my phone battery suddenly died. Without digital navigation, my heart sank. I felt a surge of panic initially, but I managed to keep my composure. I stepped into a local handicraft shop and asked an elderly artisan for directions. He kindly drew a crude map on paper and pointed me toward a main thoroughfare. Following his guidance, I successfully reached my destination, feeling relieved and proud of my spontaneous problem-solving skills."

Lexical Masterclass:

1. Labyrinthine (adjective) /ˌlæb.əˈrɪn.θaɪn/ - Intricate, complicated, or maze-like structure of paths.

2. Keep one's composure (idiom) - To remain calm and self-controlled under stressful circumstances.

3. Thoroughfare (noun) /ˈθʌr.ə.feər/ - A main road or public highway open at both ends.

Part 3 Extensions (10 Questions):

1. Is a paper map still necessary?

Ans: Yes, paper maps remain an essential failsafe backup when digital devices lose power or satellite signal. In remote areas without cellular coverage, physical maps save travelers from getting stranded. For example, mountain trekkers rely on printed topographical maps. Thus, paper maps preserve vital navigational security.

2. How do people react when they get lost?

Ans: Reactions vary from immediate anxiety to methodical orientation attempts. Unprepared travelers often panic, whereas experienced commuters systematically retrace their steps or consult locals. For instance, tourists in foreign cities usually seek help at information kiosks. Therefore, emotional resilience dictates one's response.

3. Why do some people get lost more easily than others?

Ans: Variations stem from individual differences in spatial awareness and observation habits. People who pay attention to visual landmarks navigate far better than those distracted by phones. For example, noticing distinct buildings helps memory retention. Hence, active observation prevents disorientation.

4. Do you think it is important to be able to read a map?

Ans: Absolutely, map literacy is a fundamental life skill that builds spatial reasoning and independence. Understanding cardinal directions and scales prevents dangerous navigation errors. For example, reading subway maps allows seamless transit in global cities. Consequently, map reading fosters self-reliance.

5. Do you think it is important to do some preparation before you travel to new places?

Ans: Yes, pre-trip research minimizes safety risks and unforeseen travel delays. Reviewing neighborhood layouts and downloading offline maps ensures smooth navigation upon arrival. For instance, knowing transport routes prevents airport scams. Thus, preparation guarantees enjoyable journeys.

6. How can people find their way when they are lost?

Ans: Individuals can seek assistance from local shopkeepers, spot major architectural landmarks, or retrace their steps to a known intersection. Observing public transit stops also provides orientation cues. For example, finding a metro station helps realign with city maps. Therefore, using environment cues resolves disorientation.

7. How has GPS technology changed the way people navigate?

Ans: GPS tools have completely democratized global travel by providing turn-by-turn voice prompts and real-time traffic updates. Navigating complex cities no longer requires memorizing street grids. For instance, drivers avoid traffic jams using live route re-direction. Consequently, travel anxiety is significantly reduced.

8. What are the disadvantages of relying too much on navigation apps?

Ans: Over-reliance leads to cognitive atrophy of natural direction senses and vulnerability during battery drain. Users follow screens blindly without noticing surroundings. For example, drivers occasionally turn onto unsuitable roads due to app glitches. Hence, digital dependence dulls spatial instinct.

9. Do you think people's sense of direction has gotten worse because of technology?

Ans: Yes, constant reliance on automated prompts reduces our need to build internal mental maps. Because algorithms guide every turn, people rarely practice landmark recall. For example, drivers struggle without active GPS prompts in familiar areas. Therefore, technological convenience weakens spatial skills.

10. What should parents teach their children about navigation and direction?

Ans: Parents should teach kids how to identify major landmarks, read basic neighborhood maps, and memorize emergency contact checkpoints. Engaging children in direction games during walks builds spatial confidence. For instance, asking a child to guide the walk back home builds memory. Thus, early navigation practice instills independence.

78 Describe an outdoor sport you would like to try for the first time

Part 2 Cue Card:

You should say: What it is; When/where you would like to do it; With whom you would like to do it; And explain why you would like to do it.

Band 7.5+ Sample Response:

"An outdoor sport that I have been dying to try is kayaking down the scenic waters of Ha Long Bay. I would love to go there next summer with my closest group of university friends. The sport involves paddling a small narrow watercraft through open coastal waters. I am drawn to kayaking because it offers an exhilarating adrenaline rush while allowing intimate exploration of secluded limestone caves and hidden lagoons that large cruise ships cannot access. Furthermore, it demands robust upper-body endurance, making it a perfect blend of rigorous physical exercise and peaceful natural discovery."

Lexical Masterclass:

1. Exhilarating (adjective) /ɪɡˈzɪl.ə.reɪ.tɪŋ/ - Making one feel exceptionally happy, excited, and energized.

2. Secluded (adjective) /sɪˈkluː.dɪd/ - Quiet, private, and sheltered from screen noise or human crowds.

3. Rigorous (adjective) /ˈrɪɡ.ər.əs/ - Extremely thorough, demanding, and challenging physically or mentally.

Part 3 Extensions (10 Questions):

1. What are the differences between indoor sports and outdoor sports?

Ans: Indoor sports offer climate-controlled consistency, whereas outdoor sports provide fresh air and natural terrain variety. Indoor activities like badminton avoid weather disruptions, while outdoor sports like trail running build mental resilience against elements. Thus, environment dictates the athletic experience.

2. What outdoor sports are popular in Vietnam?

Ans: Football, street badminton, and outdoor marathon running are exceptionally popular across Vietnamese cities. Public parks are packed with jogging groups every early morning. For instance, marathon events in Da Nang attract thousands of runners annually. Hence, group fitness leads outdoor trends.

3. What sports are popular among elderly people?

Ans: Seniors generally favor low-impact physical activities such as Tai Chi, brisk walking, and outdoor morning aerobics. These exercises protect fragile joints while promoting cardiovascular health. For example, elderly groups gather by lakes at dawn for gentle stretching. Therefore, low-impact movement supports senior health.

4. Which outdoor sports are popular with children?

Ans: Children naturally gravitate toward energetic activities like roller-skating, outdoor cycling, and football. These sports offer playful competition and physical energy release. For instance, neighborhood playgrounds are full of kids playing tag and cycling. Thus, dynamic games dominate youth preferences.

5. How does weather affect outdoor sports?

Ans: Weather conditions directly dictate event scheduling and athlete performance levels. Extreme heat causes rapid dehydration, while heavy rains make playing fields dangerous. For example, monsoon storms often force football match cancellations. Consequently, outdoor sports require flexible weather planning.

6. Which is more dangerous, team sports or individual sports?

Ans: Danger depends on the sport type rather than group size, though extreme individual sports carry higher severe injury risks. Contact team sports like rugby cause frequent collisions, but solo extreme sports like free-solo climbing lack immediate team safety nets. Thus, risk factors vary widely.

7. How can governments encourage more people to participate in outdoor sports?

Ans: Authorities should subsidize public sports facilities and build dedicated pedestrian bike paths in urban districts. Hosting free civic sports festivals raises health awareness. For instance, public outdoor gyms in public parks encourage daily exercise. Therefore, accessible infrastructure drives participation.

8. What equipment is necessary for different outdoor sports?

Ans: Equipment ranges from basic protective footwear to specialized gear like helmets, harnesses, and safety vests. Water sports demand flotation vests, while cycling requires certified helmets. For example, rock climbing demands high-grade ropes and carabiners. Hence, safety gear is vital across disciplines.

9. Do you think outdoor sports are more beneficial than indoor sports?

Ans: Yes, outdoor sports combine physical conditioning with natural psychological stress relief. Exercising in natural sunlight boosts vitamin D levels and mental mood better than indoor gyms. For example, outdoor trail running offers richer sensory stimulation. Consequently, outdoor fitness provides holistic benefits.

10. How has technology changed outdoor sports activities?

Ans: Wearable fitness trackers and smart fabrics have revolutionized performance monitoring and personal safety. Athletes analyze real-time heart rates and elevation data on smartwatches. For instance, GPS tracking lets solo hikers send emergency distress signals. Thus, technology enhances performance and safety.

79 Describe an unusual but interesting building you would like to visit

Part 2 Cue Card:

You should say: Where it is; What it looks like; Why you think it is unusual and interesting; And explain why you would like to visit it.

Band 7.5+ Sample Response:

"An architectural wonder that I am dying to visit is the famous 'Crazy House' (Hang Nga Guesthouse) in Da Lat. Designed by an expressionist architect, the building resembles a massive, organic banyan tree with sculptural branches, cave-like guestrooms, and winding outdoor staircases. I first discovered it in an international travel magazine highlighting avant-garde architecture. It is so unusual because it rejects traditional straight lines and symmetrical geometry completely. I want to visit it because stepping through its surreal, fairytale-like pathways offers an unmatched visual feast that pushes the boundaries of human imagination."

Lexical Masterclass:

1. Avant-garde (adjective) /ˌæv.ɒ̃ˈɡɑːd/ - Ultra-modern, experimental, and unconventional in style.

2. Expressionist (adjective) /ɪkˈspreʃ.ən.ɪst/ - Art or architecture seeking to express emotional experience rather than physical reality.

3. Visual feast (idiom) - A magnificent, pleasing sight featuring rich artistic or natural details.

Part 3 Extensions (10 Questions):

1. Do you think old buildings should be preserved?

Ans: Yes, historic structures serve as tangible cultural anchors that embody a nation's history. Preserving old buildings maintains local character and attracts heritage tourism. For example, preserving French colonial buildings in Hanoi enriches urban identity. Therefore, historic preservation protects cultural legacy.

2. Do you think a city's buildings affect its vibe or atmosphere?

Ans: Absolutely, architecture dictates a city's visual energy and pedestrian mood. Cities with open plazas and historic facades feel welcoming, whereas uniform concrete towers can feel sterile. For instance, Hoi An's yellow shopfronts create a warm, nostalgic atmosphere. Consequently, architecture defines urban identity.

3. What factors do you consider when choosing a house or an apartment?

Ans: Primary considerations include location safety, proximity to public transit, and natural ventilation efficiency. Affordability and natural lighting are also critical. For example, living near metro stops saves daily commuting time. Thus, convenience and comfort guide housing decisions.

4. Why do some people choose to build houses by themselves?

Ans: Building independently allows complete architectural customization tailored to personal family needs. It also ensures strict oversight over material quality. For instance, homeowners can install custom eco-friendly solar systems. Hence, self-building guarantees tailored quality.

5. How do modern buildings differ from traditional architecture in your country?

Ans: Modern buildings utilize glass and steel for vertical efficiency, whereas traditional homes featured wooden frames and curved tile roofs designed for tropical ventilation. Traditional layouts prioritized communal courtyards. For example, modern high-rises focus on spatial density. Thus, materials and structural concepts differ.

6. What makes a building environmentally friendly?

Ans: Sustainable buildings incorporate solar power generation, rainwater harvesting, and energy-efficient insulation. Utilizing local recycled materials reduces carbon footprints during construction. For instance, buildings with vertical gardens reduce air-conditioning needs. Therefore, eco-design minimizes environmental impact.

7. Should governments invest more in unique architectural projects?

Ans: Yes, iconic public buildings boost civic pride and serve as major tourism magnets. Distinctive cultural centers enhance international reputation. For example, Sydney's Opera House generates huge tourism revenues. Consequently, artistic public architecture yields long-term economic returns.

8. How do famous buildings contribute to tourism?

Ans: Landmark structures act as visual symbols that define travel itineraries. Travelers journey across the globe specifically to photograph iconic monuments. For example, the Eiffel Tower attracts millions to Paris annually. Thus, famous architecture powers hospitality industries.

9. What are the challenges of maintaining historical buildings?

Ans: Challenges include finding rare traditional materials, high restoration expenses, and structural weather degradation. Ancient wooden components decay without specialized treatment. For instance, historic temples require skilled artisans for restoration. Therefore, preservation demands continuous expertise and funds.

10. Do you think all cities should have some unique architectural landmarks?

Ans: Yes, landmark architecture prevents cities from becoming monotonous concrete jungles. Unique landmarks provide orientation points and foster local pride. For example, distinct bridges become city symbols. Hence, iconic landmarks enrich urban character.

80 Describe a person who helps to protect the environment

Part 2 Cue Card:

You should say: Who this person is; When this person does this; What has the person done; And explain why you think this person does this.

Band 7.5+ Sample Response:

"I would like to tell you about my inspiring university classmate, Lan, who is a passionate grassroots environmental advocate. Every single weekend, she mobilizes groups of student volunteers to organize beach cleanups and tree-planting drives along local rivers. She also founded a campus zero-waste initiative that teaches students how to compost organic waste and replace single-use plastics. I admire her deeply because she leads by example with unyielding commitment. She does this out of a profound sense of responsibility toward future generations, believing firmly that individual daily actions can spark systemic ecological change."

Lexical Masterclass:

1. Grassroots (adjective) /ˈɡrɑːs.ruːts/ - Driven by ordinary citizens at a local community level rather than political leaders.

2. Mobilize (verb) /ˈməʊ.bɪ.laɪz/ - To organize people or resources for a specific active goal or cause.

3. Lead by example (idiom) - To guide others by personal positive behavior rather than mere instructions.

Part 3 Extensions (10 Questions):

1. Why can recycling help the environment?

Ans: Recycling conserves raw natural resources and reduces landfill pollution. Processing recycled glass or aluminum consumes far less energy than extracting new materials. For example, recycling paper saves millions of trees from deforestation. Thus, recycling minimizes resource depletion.

2. What have people done to damage the environment?

Ans: Human industrial expansion has caused uncontrolled deforestation, ocean plastic contamination, and excessive fossil fuel emissions. These activities destroy natural habitats and accelerate global warming. For instance, industrial discharge pollutes major river basins. Consequently, ecosystems suffer severe degradation.

3. Do you think technology can improve environment-related problems?

Ans: Yes, green technological innovations like solar energy, electric vehicles, and carbon capture offer vital solutions. Advanced recycling technology processes plastics far more efficiently. For instance, solar technology reduces reliance on coal power plants. Therefore, green tech powers environmental recovery.

4. Is it important to teach students environmental protection at school?

Ans: Absolutely, early environmental education instills lifelong eco-conscious habits in children. Understanding ecological impact encourages responsible waste habits from a young age. For example, school recycling programs teach sorting skills early. Hence, green curricula build responsible citizens.

5. What are the most serious environmental problems facing the world today?

Ans: Climate change, global ocean pollution, and biodiversity loss are the most critical crises. Rising sea levels threaten coastal cities, while plastic waste destroys marine food chains. For instance, extreme weather disasters occur with higher frequency. Consequently, urgent global action is required.

6. How can individuals contribute to environmental protection in their daily lives?

Ans: Individuals can adopt zero-waste habits, cut household energy waste, and choose public transportation over private cars. Using reusable bags stops single-use plastic pollution. For example, commuting by metro lowers personal carbon footprints. Thus, small daily choices drive collective change.

7. Do you think governments are doing enough to protect the environment?

Ans: While many nations have signed climate pledges, progress remains slow due to short-term economic priorities. Stricter corporate emissions penalties and renewable subsidies are needed. For example, fossil fuel subsidies still hinder clean energy transitions. Hence, stronger legislative action is required.

8. What role do businesses play in environmental conservation?

Ans: Corporations must adopt sustainable supply chain models and minimize industrial manufacturing waste. Transitioning to eco-friendly packaging significantly reduces global plastic pollution. For instance, major brands replacing plastic cups with paper cuts tons of waste. Therefore, corporate responsibility is crucial.

9. How can we encourage more people to care about environmental issues?

Ans: Media campaigns illustrating local environmental impacts make climate crises feel immediate rather than distant. Financial incentives like recycling tax rebates also motivate green behavior. For example, charging for plastic shopping bags encourages cloth bag usage. Thus, education combined with incentives works best.

10. What will happen if we don't take action to protect the environment now?

Ans: Inaction will lead to catastrophic climate displacement, severe food shortages, and irreversible ecosystem collapses. Coastal cities risk flooding while agricultural yields drop sharply. For instance, extreme droughts threaten global farming security. Consequently, delayed action jeopardizes human survival.

81 Describe an important old thing that your family has kept for a long time

Part 2 Cue Card:

You should say: What it is; How/when your family first got this thing; How long your family has kept it; Why this thing is important to your family.

Band 7.5+ Sample Response:

"A treasured item that my family has preserved for over seventy years is my great-grandfather's antique mechanical pocket watch. He acquired it in the late 1940s while working as a schoolmaster. The watch features an intricate silver casing and hand-carved Roman numerals. This timepiece is an invaluable family heirloom because it symbolizes our family's perseverance through historical hardships. Looking at its ticking hands offers a deeply grounding reminder of my ancestors' values, proving that certain physical objects possess an irreplaceable sentimental value that modern gadgets cannot replicate."

Lexical Masterclass:

1. Heirloom (noun) /ˈeə.luːm/ - A valuable object passed down through several generations of a family.

2. Sentimental value (noun phrase) - The emotional worth an object holds due to personal memories rather than its price tag.

3. Timepiece (noun) /ˈtaɪm.piːs/ - An instrument for measuring time, especially a clock or watch.

Part 3 Extensions (11 Questions):

1. What kind of old things do people in your country like to keep?

Ans: In Vietnam, families often preserve vintage photo albums, antique wooden furniture, traditional lacquerware, and inherited jewelry. These items carry ancestral memories and heritage. For example, family altars often showcase multi-generational brass incense burners. Thus, heirlooms connect generations.

2. Why do people keep old things?

Ans: People keep vintage objects due to deep nostalgic attachment and historical value. Physical items evoke specific memories of departed relatives far better than digital photos. For example, holding a grandmother's hand-written recipe book brings comfort. Hence, old items preserve emotional continuity.

3. What are the differences between the things old people keep and those young people keep?

Ans: Seniors hold onto traditional physical heirlooms like letters and clocks, whereas youth preserve digital archives, vintage fashion, or collectible vinyl records. Older generations value historical permanence, while youth value aesthetic trends. For example, teens collect retro cameras. Thus, generational values differ.

4. What are the differences between the things that people keep today and the things that people kept in the past?

Ans: Past generations kept durable, hand-crafted utilitarian goods built to last lifetimes, whereas modern populations store digital files or mass-produced goods. High-quality craftsmanship was prioritized historically. For instance, antique furniture lasted centuries unlike flat-pack items. Consequently, durability has declined.

5. What can we see in a museum?

Ans: Museums showcase archaeological artifacts, historical military gear, fine art, and ancient manuscripts. These collections document human cultural evolution across eras. For instance, history museums display ancient bronze drums. Therefore, museums archive human history.

6. What can we learn from a museum?

Ans: Museums offer contextual historical insights into societal triumphs, artistic movements, and past struggles. Seeing physical historical artifacts deepens textbook knowledge. For example, visiting war memorials teaches resilience lessons. Thus, museums provide immersive education.

7. Do you think it's important for families to pass on old things to the next generation?

Ans: Yes, passing down heirlooms instills family pride and preserves ancestral identity. Inherited objects keep family oral histories alive across centuries. For instance, receiving an ancestor's ring builds belonging. Therefore, heirlooms strengthen family continuity.

8. How do old objects help us understand history?

Ans: Artifacts provide tangible evidence of ancient manufacturing techniques, daily domestic habits, and trade networks. Examining ancient tools reveals technical capacities of past societies. For example, ancient coins illuminate historical trade routes. Hence, objects validate history.

9. Are there any risks or disadvantages to keeping old things?

Ans: Excessive hoarding leads to cluttered living spaces and potential maintenance burdens. Old items deteriorate without costly climate-controlled storage. For example, ancient textiles decay if exposed to humidity. Thus, keeping antiques requires careful care.

10. How do people usually take care of valuable old things?

Ans: Valuables are stored in protective display cases, maintained with specialized cleaning solutions, or kept in climate-controlled environments. High-value antiques are often professionally restored. For instance, old oil paintings undergo varnish restoration. Therefore, preservation requires meticulous upkeep.

11. In what ways can old things be preserved for the future?

Ans: Beyond physical museum conservation, 3D scanning and digital archiving allow eternal virtual preservation. High-resolution digital catalogs ensure historical knowledge survives physical decay. For example, ancient manuscripts are scanned for online access. Thus, digitization secures heritage.

82 Describe a natural talent (sports, music, etc.) you want to improve

Part 2 Cue Card:

You should say: What it is; When you discovered it; How you want to improve it; And how you feel about it.

Band 7.5+ Sample Response:

"A natural gift that I am eager to systematically refine is my ability to play piano melodies by ear. I first discovered this talent in primary school when I picked out simple tunes on a keyboard without sheet music. While I have an intuitive pitch sense, I lack formal music theory mastery. I plan to improve this by taking professional jazz piano lessons and practicing structured ear-training exercises daily. I feel immensely grateful for this gift because music acts as my ultimate creative outlet, allowing me to express emotions authentically through harmonies."

Lexical Masterclass:

1. Intuitive (adjective) /ɪnˈtʃuː.ɪ.tɪv/ - Based on natural instinct or feelings rather than conscious reasoning.

2. Ear-training (noun phrase) - Exercises helping musicians identify pitches, intervals, and chords by hearing.

3. Creative outlet (noun phrase) - An activity letting one express feelings and imaginative energy freely.

Part 3 Extensions (9 Questions):

1. Do you think artists with talents should focus on their talents?

Ans: Yes, gifted individuals should nurture their abilities through dedicated practice to reach artistic excellence. Raw talent requires structured training to produce meaningful artistic works. For instance, natural painters still master technique. Thus, focus turns raw potential into mastery.

2. Why do people like to watch talent shows?

Ans: Talent shows offer engaging entertainment and inspire audiences through compelling underdog stories. Viewers enjoy witnessing ordinary citizens achieve overnight success. For example, singing competitions captivate millions of television viewers. Consequently, talent shows offer emotional inspiration.

3. Do you think it is more interesting to watch famous people's or ordinary people's shows?

Ans: Watching ordinary people is far more authentic and relatable, as audiences witness raw undiscovered talent. Celebrity shows often feel rehearsed, whereas amateur performances display genuine emotion. For example, unknown singers winning competitions creates viral excitement. Hence, amateur talent feels more authentic.

4. How important is family support in developing a natural talent?

Ans: Family support is vital for providing financial tuition resources and emotional encouragement during early childhood practice. Without parental guidance, young talent often goes undiscovered. For instance, young athletes depend on parents for training transport. Therefore, family support fuels talent development.

5. Should talented individuals receive special opportunities or treatment in education?

Ans: Yes, gifted students benefit from tailored academies that accelerate their specific skill sets without neglecting core academics. Specialized sports or music schools help prodigies excel. For example, conservatory training develops elite musicians. Thus, specialized tracks maximize potential.

6. What challenges do talented people often face in their careers?

Ans: Challenges include intense public pressure, burnout from perfectionism, and fierce commercial competition. High early expectations create severe performance anxiety. For example, child prodigies occasionally struggle with adult career transitions. Consequently, mental resilience is required alongside talent.

7. Can hard work compensate for a lack of natural talent?

Ans: Absolutely, deliberate practice and discipline often outperform unpracticed raw talent over the long run. Consistent effort builds technical mastery that surpasses effortless initial ability. As the saying goes, hard work beats talent when talent doesn't work hard. Thus, grit guarantees success.

8. How do cultural values influence the recognition of talent?

Ans: Cultural priorities determine which talents receive prestige and financial backing. Some societies celebrate academic or athletic excellence, while others value artistic or musical mastery. For instance, sports talents receive huge sponsorships in Western markets. Therefore, culture shapes talent recognition.

9. In what ways can society encourage the development of young people's talents?

Ans: Communities can subsidize youth art programs, build public sports complexes, and host regional talent competitions. Accessible grants allow underprivileged youth to pursue training. For example, public music workshops expose kids to instruments early. Hence, public funding nurtures future talent.
