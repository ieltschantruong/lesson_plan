2. Speaking Part 2 & 3 Practice

Topic Resource List

Strategic Guidance: Band 5.5 to 6.5 Upgrade in Art & History

When discussing abstract historical developments or artistic concepts, Band 5.5 students often pause due to a lack of specialized words. Guide the student to utilize clear, natural structures:

  • Part 2 Monologues: Use chronological past markers (*"In the 20th century...", "Before I studied this event...", "What impressed me most was..."*).
  • Part 3 A.R.E.A Framework: Direct Answer [A] → Reason [R] → Example [E] → Alternative/Conditional [A].
  • Accessible Lexis: Focus on practical collocations (*"preserve heritage"*, *"cultural identity"*, *"historical significance"*, *"creative freedom"*, *"digital artwork"*).

Part 2 Cue Cards (Click to inspect Band 6.5 Model Stories)

CUE CARD A

Describe a historical event you find interesting.

  • • What the event was and when it happened?
  • • Where it took place?
  • • What happened during the event?
  • • And explain why you find this historical event interesting.
CUE CARD B

Describe a creative person whose work you admire.

  • • Who this person is?
  • • What kind of creative work they do?
  • • How you first learned about their work?
  • • And explain why you admire this person's creative work.

Part 3 Discussion Questions (Click to expand A.R.E.A Model Answers)

[A - Answer] Yes, I strongly believe that basic art education should be compulsory for primary and secondary students.

[R - Reason] Art classes help children develop creative thinking skills and relieve academic stress from subjects like math or science.

[E - Example] For example, drawing or painting allows young students to express their emotions and improve their focus.

[A - Alternative] If schools focus purely on academic exams, students might become overly stressed and lack imagination.

[A - Answer] Technology has given creators digital tools that make designing much faster and more versatile.

[R - Reason] Instead of using traditional physical paints and canvases, many modern artists now work on graphic tablets or use software programs.

[E - Example] For instance, digital illustrators can undo mistakes easily and share their artwork instantly across global social media platforms.

[A - Alternative] However, traditional painting techniques remain deeply valued by art collectors who appreciate physical craftsmanship.

[A - Answer] In my view, wealthy collectors spend huge sums mainly because of the artwork's rarity and investment potential.

[R - Reason] Original masterpieces by famous historical artists are extremely scarce, so owning them brings social prestige and financial value.

[E - Example] For instance, paintings by Van Gogh or Da Vinci increase in value over time, making them safe assets for rich investors.

[A - Alternative] On the other hand, average buyers simply purchase prints or local artwork for home decoration at modest prices.

[A - Answer] I think entrance to public museums should ideally be free so everyone can access national culture.

[R - Reason] Free admission encourages students and lower-income families to learn about history and heritage without financial barriers.

[E - Example] For instance, major state museums in London offer free entry, attracting millions of local students every year.

[A - Alternative] However, if governments cannot fund them, charging a small, affordable ticket fee helps maintain the facilities.

[A - Answer] Studying historical errors teaches us how to prevent devastating wars and protect global peace.

[R - Reason] History shows the heavy human and economic costs of armed conflicts and social injustice.

[E - Example] For example, understanding the destruction caused by World War II led nations to establish international peace organizations.

[A - Alternative] If society ignores these historical lessons, we risk repeating similar political or environmental catastrophes.

[A - Answer] Modern history education relies much more on interactive media rather than memorizing dry dates from textbooks.

[R - Reason] Teachers now use documentary films, virtual museum tours, and interactive games to make lessons engaging.

[E - Example] For instance, high school students today watch 3D historical reconstructions instead of just reading long chapters.

[A - Alternative] Without these engaging digital tools, teenagers often consider history to be a boring subject.

[A - Answer] Yes, I believe historical relics should be returned to their countries of origin whenever possible.

[R - Reason] These artifacts represent national identity, and returning them shows moral respect for a nation's heritage.

[E - Example] For example, several Western museums are currently returning ancient statues back to Greece and African nations.

[A - Alternative] However, if the origin country lacks secure facilities, temporary loans with shared ownership might be a better compromise.
